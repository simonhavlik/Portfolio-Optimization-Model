Vision/roadmap

Your portfolio, and the funds
you measure it against.

This began as a risk tool for one book: upload it, get the frontier, the drawdown, the tail. The leaderboard pushed it outward — the same measurements applied to other people's portfolios, rebuilt from their 13F filings, so a fund can be read the way you read your own. What is still manual is the ingestion: ten funds chosen by hand. The plan on this page is to automate that, so any fund that files is compared on identical inputs and refreshed without anyone touching a spreadsheet.

01

Why compare at all

A Sharpe ratio on its own says nothing. On the book you hold, it says what you gave up by holding it. Against a fund you admire, it says whether the trade was worth making. The comparison is the product; the metric is only the unit.

02

What is being compared

Quarterly 13F filings: long US equity, disclosed values, a 45-day lag. Weights are taken at each filing and chained from its filing date, then priced daily. What you see is the return of the book the manager actually disclosed — not a backtest of a strategy he describes.

03

Where it is going

Every active filer ingested on a schedule, not ten chosen by hand. Cohorts by size and style, so a $400m manager is not ranked against Berkshire. A per-fund page as detailed as the one you get for your own book.

The path a filing takes

Six stages, each one already code. The work left is removing the assumptions that keep it at ten funds.

Filing side — EDGAR, no API key, 10 requests a second Market side — daily closes, cached a week

Roadmap

Written as engineering, in order. Each phase removes a manual step rather than adding a feature.

  1. shippedSingle-filer pipelineEDGAR → XML → CUSIP map → weighted book
  2. shippedTwenty-quarter chainingweights apply from the filing date
  3. nextMass ingestionevery active filer, incremental by quarter
  4. nextAutomatic validationcoverage, amendments, orphan CUSIPs
  5. laterUniverse rankingcohorts by size and style, not by hand
  6. laterScheduled publicationrebuild, export, notify

The work

What each phase actually requires, and what it unlocks.

Shipped

  • Filings pulled per CIK, information tables parsed namespace-agnostically
  • CUSIPs mapped to tickers; coverage reported per filing, not assumed
  • Books chained so a weight never applies before its filing date
  • Ten funds ranked on ten measures, with a per-fund page and CSV export

Next — mass ingestion

  • Walk every active 13F filer instead of a curated list
  • Incremental pulls: newest quarter only, cached by accession number
  • Amendments deduplicated by period; restatements preferred over originals
  • Validation gates: coverage below threshold, unmapped CUSIPs, value jumps
  • Throttled at the SEC's rate limit with backoff, so a crawl cannot be banned

Later — cohorts & cadence

  • Cohorts by AUM and style, so a $400m manager is not ranked against Berkshire
  • Persistence: does the ranking survive a rolling window, or is it one good year
  • Book-level factor exposure — what a manager actually owns, not what he says
  • Scheduled rebuild overnight, exports regenerated, changes diffed quarter on quarter

Constraints

  • 45-day lag13Fs arrive a month and a half after quarter end; the board is a record, not a signal.
  • Long US equity onlyNo shorts, options, cash or non-US lines are disclosed, so a book is a floor on exposure.
  • Ratios, not sharesWeights come from disclosed values; splits and corporate actions are handled through the price series.
  • No back-fillEvery metric is computed from filings public at the time, or it would be look-ahead and worthless.
My friends open one page, see where each fund stands against the others, and argue about it with the numbers in front of them — while the pipeline refreshes itself every quarter. That is the whole ambition: the comparison tool we kept building by hand.
