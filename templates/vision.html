Vision/build plan

Ten funds is a proof.
The next version ingests every filing.

I built this for myself and four friends who were sizing positions off a spreadsheet and a hunch. It works: any of us can upload a book and get the frontier, the tail risk and the report in about a minute. The bottleneck is no longer the analytics — it is the data pipeline. Everything below is about removing the human from the 13F side of it, so the same comparison runs on every fund that files instead of the ten I wired by hand.

  • Ten funds live
  • Twenty quarters chained
  • 99.8% of each book mapped
  • 45-day filing lag

01

What runs now

EDGAR submissions give the filing list; the information table gives positions and values; CUSIPs are mapped to tickers through OpenFIGI; a book is weighted, chained quarter by quarter from the filing date, and priced. Twenty quarters per fund, ten funds, one board.

02

What is still done by hand

Choosing the ten funds. Reading a coverage report when a CUSIP fails to map. Deciding whether a new filing is an amendment or a restatement. Adding a fund to the registry. Four decisions that should be rules, not judgement.

03

What that costs

Scale. The pipeline is correct but narrow: it answers a question about ten funds that I chose. The point of automating the ingestion is to make the choice unnecessary — every filer, ranked on the same evidence, refreshed without me.

The path a filing takes

Six stages, each one already code. The work left is removing the assumptions that keep it at ten funds.

Filing side — EDGAR, no API key, 10 requests a second Market side — daily closes, cached a week

Roadmap

Written as engineering, in order. Each phase removes a manual step rather than adding a feature.

  1. shippedSingle-filer pipelineEDGAR → XML → CUSIP map → weighted book
  2. shippedTwenty-quarter chainingweights apply from the filing date
  3. buildingMass ingestionevery active filer, incremental by quarter
  4. buildingAutomatic validationcoverage, amendments, orphan CUSIPs
  5. nextUniverse rankingcohorts by size and style, not by hand
  6. nextScheduled publicationrebuild, export, notify

The work

What each phase actually requires, and what it unlocks.

Phase 1 — shipped

  • Filings pulled per CIK, information tables parsed namespace-agnostically
  • CUSIPs mapped to tickers; coverage reported per filing, not assumed
  • Books chained so a weight never applies before its filing date
  • Ten funds ranked on ten measures, with a per-fund page and CSV export

Phase 2 — building

  • Walk every active 13F filer instead of a curated list
  • Incremental pulls: newest quarter only, cached by accession number
  • Amendments deduplicated by period; restatements preferred over originals
  • Validation gates: coverage below threshold, unmapped CUSIPs, value jumps
  • Throttled at the SEC's rate limit with backoff, so a crawl cannot be banned

Phase 3 — next

  • Cohorts by AUM and style, so a $400m manager is not ranked against Berkshire
  • Persistence: does the ranking survive a rolling window, or is it one good year
  • Book-level factor exposure — what a manager actually owns, not what he says
  • Scheduled rebuild overnight, exports regenerated, changes diffed quarter on quarter

Constraints

  • 45-day lag13Fs arrive a month and a half after quarter end; the board is a record, not a signal.
  • Long US equity onlyNo shorts, options, cash or non-US lines are disclosed, so a book is a floor on exposure.
  • Ratios, not sharesWeights come from disclosed values; splits and corporate actions are handled through the price series.
  • No back-fillEvery metric is computed from filings public at the time, or it would be look-ahead and worthless.
The endpoint is simple: my friends open one page, see where each fund stands against the others, and argue about it with the numbers in front of them — while the pipeline refreshes itself every quarter.
