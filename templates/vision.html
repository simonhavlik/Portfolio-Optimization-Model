Vision/2026 — 2029

A risk desk for everyone
who manages money without one.

This started as my own question: what does my book actually look like once its own volatility is priced in? It is now a system that reads a portfolio the way an institution would — exposures, tail risk, regime behaviour — and answers in seconds, in language someone can act on.

  • Built in Prague
  • Running live
  • Ten funds ranked
  • Forty metrics per book

01

The asymmetry

Institutions run risk desks: people, systems and a mandate to ask uncomfortable questions before capital moves. Private investors run intuition, an app and a spreadsheet that has not been opened since spring. The gap is not information. Information is free. The gap is instrumentation.

02

The instrument

One upload returns the desk's output: correlation structure, drawdown behaviour, CVaR at 95, factor beta, regime-conditional exposure — computed on the book you actually hold, not a model portfolio that happens to look good in a deck.

03

The frontier

Not the backtest that flatters. The frontier you can defend: constraints you can explain to a client, worst cases you can survive, and a report you can hand to someone else without rewriting the caveats.

The system

Five layers, one loop. Every layer exists because a question could not be answered at the layer above it.

Left — what goes in Right — what comes out Centre — the loop that ties them

Six stages from a spreadsheet to a decision. Everything is computed; nothing is asserted.

2026 — 2029

Three horizons. The first is running, the second is in build, the third is the point.

Now

  • Multi-asset intake with parameters that travel with the report
  • Leaderboard: ten funds, chained 13F books, eleven exports
  • Excel and PDF that a client can read without you in the room

Next

  • Factor attribution: which exposures paid, which were paid for
  • Regime-conditional allocation, tested on your book not a textbook
  • Benchmark construction and a public API for the analytics

Beyond

  • Household view: accounts, mandates and currencies in one risk picture
  • Allocator workspaces — diligence, monitoring, and quarterly letters
  • An instrument small funds use instead of hiring their first risk analyst
Risk is not volatility. It is the distance between the drawdown you planned for and the one you meet.
